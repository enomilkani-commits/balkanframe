History of Balkan cinema, 1905–1999

From the Manaki brothers' first camera to the end of the century, and where to watch the classics today.

The story in brief

In 1905 Yanaki and Milton Manaki, photographers from Bitola, bought a Bioscope camera in London and filmed their 114-year-old grandmother at her loom in Avdella. The Weavers is regarded as the first motion picture shot in the Balkans. Over the next decade the first national features followed: Karađorđe in Serbia (1911), Independența României in Romania (1912), Golfo in Greece (1914) and The Gallant Bulgarian in Bulgaria (1915).

After the Second World War, state studios shaped the region: Yugoslavia's republican studios in Belgrade, Zagreb, Ljubljana, Sarajevo, Skopje and Titograd; Kinostudio Shqipëria e Re in Tirana (from 1952, first feature Tana in 1958); Boyana in Bulgaria and Buftea in Romania. Greek cinema flourished in Athens with Cacoyannis and later Angelopoulos, and Kosovafilm was founded in Prishtina in 1969.

The 1960s Yugoslav "Black Wave", Romanian and Bulgarian auteurs and Albanian Kinostudio classics brought the region to Cannes, Venice, Berlin and the Oscars. The century closed with Palmes d'Or for Kusturica (1985, 1995) and Angelopoulos (1998), and a Golden Lion for Manchevski's Before the Rain (1994).
